title: Was Iran months from a nuclear weapon, and had it begun to weaponize?

sequence: |
  [DOCUMENTED] 2003: Iran suspended its nuclear weapons program, as the US intelligence community still assessed in March 2025. (CON-002)

  [DOCUMENTED] May 8, 2018: The United States ceased participation in the JCPOA nuclear agreement (National Security Presidential Memorandum 11).
  Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-201800311/html/DCPD-201800311.htm

  [DOCUMENTED] July 10, 2023: An ODNI assessment under the Iran Nuclear Weapons Capability and Terrorism Monitoring Act stated: "Iran is not currently undertaking the key nuclear weapons-development activities that would be necessary to produce a testable nuclear device."
  Source: ODNI, Iran's Nuclear Weapons Capability and Terrorism Monitoring Act of 2022, June 2023 (released 10 July 2023) — https://www.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/Iran-Nuclear-Weapons-Capability-and-Terrorism-Monitoring-Act-of-2022.pdf

  [DOCUMENTED] July 2024: The next assessment under the same law no longer contains that sentence. It says Iran is better positioned to produce a nuclear device "if it chooses to do so" and notes "a notable increase this year in Iranian public statements about nuclear weapons." (SUP-003)

  [DOCUMENTED] March 25, 2025: The US Director of National Intelligence testified that Iran "is not building a nuclear weapon" and that its enriched uranium stockpile is "unprecedented for a state without nuclear weapons." (CON-002)

  [DOCUMENTED] May 31, 2025: The IAEA reported 408.6 kg of uranium enriched up to 60% as of 17 May 2025 (SUP-001) and "no credible indications of an ongoing, undeclared structured nuclear programme" (CON-001).

  [DOCUMENTED] June 13, 2025: Israel began strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities. The IAEA stopped verification activities that day. In a recorded address announcing the operation, Netanyahu said Iran could produce a nuclear weapon within "a year" or "a few months" and had recently taken "steps to weaponize" its enriched uranium. (The claim; GOV/2025/50 paragraph 8)

  [DOCUMENTED] June 14, 2025: In a statement released by the Prime Minister's Office, Netanyahu said Israel had "taken out their senior technologists who are leading the race to build atomic weapons."
  Source: https://www.gov.il/en/pages/statement-by-pm-netanyahu-14-jun-2025

  [DOCUMENTED] June 20, 2025: IAEA Director General Grossi said Iran had "enough material for several warheads" but that the IAEA had no "tangible proof that there is a program, or a plan" to manufacture a nuclear weapon. (SUP-002, CON-003)

  [DOCUMENTED] June 22, 2025: The United States struck Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan (Operation Midnight Hammer) with seven B-2 bombers and more than two dozen Tomahawk missiles. The Secretary of Defense said the operation "was designed to severely degrade Iran's nuclear weapons infrastructure."
  Source: US Department of Defense press briefing, 22 June 2025 — https://www.war.gov/News/Transcripts/Transcript/Article/4222543/secretary-of-defense-pete-hegseth-and-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-gen/

  [DOCUMENTED] June 24, 2025: The IAEA reported "no radiological impact to the population and the environment in neighbouring countries" and said inspectors had last verified "more than 400 kg of uranium enriched to 60%" a few days before the strikes began.
  Source: IAEA, Update on Developments in Iran (6) — https://www.iaea.org/newscenter/pressreleases/update-on-developments-in-iran-6

  [DOCUMENTED] By the end of June 2025, the IAEA withdrew all inspectors from Iran. On July 2, 2025, Iran enacted a law suspending cooperation with the IAEA. The IAEA's September report put Iran's 60% stockpile at 440.9 kg as of 13 June and said the Agency had "lost continuity of knowledge" of Iran's enriched uranium inventories.
  Source: IAEA, GOV/2025/50, 3 September 2025, paragraphs 8, 36 and 39 — https://www.iaea.org/sites/default/files/documents/gov2025-50.pdf

  [INFERENCE] The quantity of highly enriched uranium in the claim is supported by the IAEA. The claim that Iran had recently begun to weaponize is not supported by any public independent assessment: the IAEA (31 May) and the US intelligence community (25 March) each said, before the strikes, that they saw no weapons programme, and the IAEA head said the same a week into the strikes. The "a year … a few months" timeline cannot be tested from public sources.

  [INFERENCE] Israel said it acted on its own intelligence, which has not been published. If it shows weaponization work after March–May 2025, it would be material to this assessment. Contradiction between public assessments and the claim does not establish what the speaker knew or intended.
gap_summary: On the day Israel struck Iran, Netanyahu justified the strikes with
  three claims. The first, that Iran held enough highly enriched uranium for
  several bombs, is supported by IAEA figures (408.6 kg at 60% in May 2025).
  The second, that Iran had recently taken steps to weaponize it, is
  contradicted by every public independent assessment available at the time;
  the IAEA found no credible indications of a structured weapons programme two
  weeks earlier, and the US intelligence community assessed in March 2025 that
  Iran was not building a weapon. The third, that Iran could have a weapon
  within a year or a few months, has no public independent assessment to test
  it against. The gap lies in the weaponization claim, which rests on
  intelligence that has not been made public.
